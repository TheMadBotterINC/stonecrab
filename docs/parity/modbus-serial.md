# Modbus RTU and ASCII parity record
Baseline: `upstream/manifest.toml`, Apache PLC4X v1.0.0 (tag `v1.0.0`),
commit `ea229918f5d07700e04d7d69ab1645da679cb700`.

State at S60b: Modbus RTU (S59) and Modbus ASCII (S60) are implemented (ADR-0024 and its
addendum S60): the `modbus-rtu` and `modbus-ascii` drivers (`ModbusRtuDriver`,
`ModbusAsciiDriver`) over the serial transport of S58 or a TCP gateway, RTU framing with the
CRC-16 (`RtuFramer`, `RtuAdu`) and ASCII framing with the LRC (`AsciiFramer`, `AsciiAdu`),
the silence before each RTU request and the drain after an unanswered request of either,
correlation by unit and function code, and the shared session, connection and PDU codec of
Modbus TCP. The rows below say `implemented`, `deferred` or a decided divergence, with their
test ids; the evidence is simulator level (the fake transport and the fake clock, the
contract suite, the upstream RTU and ASCII vectors, fuzz targets and RTU and ASCII over
loopback TCP), with no hardware yet. Since S59b the RTU half of the rows the `interop-rtu`
job exercises is also checked against an independent peer: diagslave 3.6 run as Modbus RTU
over TCP, which `modbus-rtu:tcp://` speaks (`interop/diagslave/`, nine `interop_rtu_*` tests
in `modbus_interop_diagslave_rtu`). Since S60b the same image also runs diagslave as a
Modbus ASCII slave (`-m ascii`) and as a Modbus RTU slave (`-m rtu`) on a serial device, one
end of a pseudo-terminal pair that socat makes, the other end bridged to a TCP port that the
drivers' gateway carrier reaches: nine `interop_ascii_*` tests
(`modbus_interop_diagslave_ascii`, job `interop-ascii`) and the `interop_rtu_*` tests again
(job `interop-rtu-pty`), so diagslave's ASCII framing and LRC and its RTU framing as it does
it on a line are met too. The three jobs passed locally on 2026-10-29 (colima, 9 of 9 each);
the first hosted runs are the next evidence. What none of them is: a serial port of the
driver (the carrier is `tcp`, so `serial.*` options, the silence before a request and the
reopen of a lost port are not exercised) or a device. A row shared by both drivers says
which halves are done. (S52d added the `MBS-DEVICE-SLM1C-*` rows of "Hardware evidence": a
replay of the SLM1-C's documented register map at the PDU level, over TCP framing until S52e
put it on RTU.) The PDU codec, the address grammar, the read batcher, the typed decoder, the
byte-order modes and the exception mapping are shared with Modbus TCP and are not restated
here: this record points at `docs/parity/modbus.md` (rows `MB-*`) for them and lists only
what differs on a serial line. The manifest entries are `inventory.modbus_rtu`,
`inventory.modbus_ascii` and `inventory.transport_serial`; the non-Java drivers are
`go_modbus`, `c_modbus` and `python_modbus`.

Status vocabulary, as used in the Status column (the rest, including
`implemented (divergence decided <slice>)` and `; rejection implemented`, is defined in
`docs/parity/modbus.md`):

- `planned (Mn)`: Stonecrab will port the behavior in milestone Mn. The Test ids column
  names at least one test id prefixed `planned:`; none exists yet.
- `deferred (<reason>)`: Stonecrab does not port the behavior now. The reason is a
  non-Java-only capability, an upstream placeholder, or a decision. Test ids may be `none`.
- `implemented (S59, RTU)`: the RTU half exists and the listed tests pass; `; S60, ASCII`
  after it adds the ASCII half of a shared row, which the listed ASCII tests cover.
- `automated-verified (S59b, ...)` and `automated-verified (S60b, ...)`: the RTU half of the
  row is also checked against diagslave 3.6 by the `interop-rtu` job (`-m enc`) and, since
  S60b, the `interop-rtu-pty` job (`-m rtu` on a serial device), and the ASCII half by the
  `interop-ascii` job (`-m ascii` on a serial device); every job reaches diagslave through the
  drivers' TCP gateway carrier (`modbus-rtu:tcp://`, `modbus-ascii:tcp://`) and never through
  a serial port of the driver; the rest of the status says what else the row holds. The other
  rows the `interop_rtu_*` and `interop_ascii_*` tests exercise (`MBS-OP-WRITE-EXT`,
  `MBS-RTU-SIZE-TABLE`, `MBS-LIMIT-ADU`, `MBS-LIMIT-ASCII-BUFFER`, `MBS-BUS-EXCEPTION`,
  `MBS-BUS-CORRELATION`, `MBS-ADDR-UNIT-ID`, `MBS-ASCII-DELIMITERS`, `MBS-ASCII-PARSE`) list
  their tests and stay `implemented`, because the peer covers only part of what they say (an
  exception for the file-record functions, one unit, no function 0x14 or 0x15 reply, one
  reply format).
- `hardware-verified`: not used yet. These rows will name the device model and firmware (HW2).

Row ids use the prefix `MBS-`. Ids are never renumbered. `scripts/parity-check.sh` does not
require the planned ids to appear in code.

## Configuration
Driver options (`RtuCfg:29-67`, `AscCfg:29-67`, identical for both): six, the same as
`ModbusTcpConfiguration`. Serial options come from the shared `SerialTransportConfiguration`
(`SerCfg:28-123`); `RtuSer` and `AscSer` add nothing (`RtuSer:19-25`). Transport options
are read under the transport code as a prefix: the serial options in a connection string are
`serial.baud-rate`, `serial.data-bits` and so on (DB:197-200; the upstream manual tests write
`serial.baud-rate=9600`, `ManualModbusRTUDriverTest.java:73`). The option names in the rows
below are written without the prefix. The driver options are unprefixed.

### Documented device map: SOLO SLM1-C (S52d)
The SLM1-C is the HW2 device: a Modbus RTU and ASCII server on RS-485. Its map is a hand
transcription of the vendor's manual (SOLO Modular Temperature Controller User Manual, 1st
Edition 07/2023, `https://cdn.automationdirect.com/static/manuals/soloslm/soloslmman.pdf`,
sha256 `830b99162dfbab23ed87c1778428747e65ce658dbce48e76899d4b948295d7cc`, retrieved
2026-10-28; vendor documentation, all rights reserved, pinned and never copied) in
`fixtures/modbus/devices/slm1-c.toml`: 58 register entries and five bit registers, with
the manual's number, access, type, default and meaning for each. The profile is
`profiles/documented/modbus-rtu/solo_slm1_c.toml`, scheme `modbus-rtu`, with the line it
records in its options (S52e: 9600 baud, 8 data bits, even parity, one stop bit, network
address 1). Both carry the label **documented map, not hardware-verified**: no device was
read. The suite (`crates/stonecrab/tests/modbus_device_replays.rs`, module `slm1_c`) runs
`ModbusRtuDriver` over the fake transport and clock with RTU framing and CRC-16 (written
independently of the crate's) against the first-party responder, with the manual's function
table and limits enforced: it proves the register map, the limits, the profile and the RTU
framing at the manual's settings, not the electrical line. The runbook
`docs/hardware/hw2-slm1-c.md` lists the steps and every item the manual leaves open (exception
codes, oversize requests, extension-module addressing).

## UNVERIFIED
- The Modbus serial line specification: the maximum RTU frame size (MBS-LIMIT-ADU), the
  3.5-character timing and the CRC and LRC definitions are stated from the specification,
  not from the manifest. A copy is not in the repository. The S44 attempt to download the
  Modbus Application Protocol V1.1b3 PDF from www.modbus.org was blocked (HTTP 403), so no
  revision has been checked and no sha256 exists; the section numbers cited for the PDU
  (in `docs/parity/modbus.md`) stay UNVERIFIED for the same reason.
- The generated `rtuCrcCheck` helper was not opened upstream. The `asciiLrcCheck` helper was read
  in S60 (Java `StaticHelper.asciiLrcCheck` and Go's port, MBS-ASCII-LRC); the generated
  parser's call to it was not.
- The ASCII details taken from the serial line specification: the 513-character maximum
  frame (MBS-LIMIT-ASCII-BUFFER; it agrees with upstream's `MODBUS_ASCII_MAX_SIZE`), upper-case
  hex, and the remembered allowance of up to a second between two characters
  (MBS-ASCII-TIMING). The specification is not checked in.
- Whether the Go driver's serial transport applies the same value normalisation as the Java
  one (not read in S44).
- Whether the `test` transport and `udp` work for RTU at runtime.
- The exact serial connection-string form for a port name. The transport passes the URL
  through as the port name (`SerT:34-61`); the manifest cites no example.
- Which versions of the line-state behavior (DTR and RTS) the platform serial layers apply
  on open; the Go platform files were listed, not read.
